## Question

Define the upgrade catalogue against the core loop: e.g. capacity, door speed, music tiers (patience), language lessons (5 per language, see the language-guessing ticket), call-panel improvements. Price curve vs. expected earnings per shift.

## Resolution

**Earnings baseline:** a near-nothing **minimum wage per shift** (around €10) plus €2 fare per guest and a tip of up to €6. With ~30 guests a beginner earns ≈ €80 per shift, a skilled player ≈ €180. Prices are tuned to that.

**Catalogue (first hotel):**

| Upgrade | Effect | Levels | Prices |
|---|---|---|---|
| Bigger cab | capacity 3 → 4 → 5 | 2 | €150 / €400 |
| Faster doors | doors open and close faster | 2 | €100 / €250 |
| Faster motor | the elevator moves faster between floors | 2 | €200 / €500 |
| Music | **starts silent.** L1 installs a music box/speakers, then better music; each level adds patience during the ride | 3 | €80 / €200 / €450 |
| Call panel+ | shows how many people wait per floor, then how impatient they are | 2 | €120 / €300 |
| Spanish lessons | see the language-guessing ticket | 5 | €60 / €100 / €150 / €220 / €300 |

- **Shop:** a staff room between shifts, never during a shift.
- **Visible:** every upgrade shows up physically in the cab (speaker, bigger cab, new panel).
- **Moving to a new hotel:** languages and music carry over; hardware (cab, doors, motor, panel) starts over at the new hotel, with higher prices. Open: whether a new hotel's elevator can come with music built in (raised by the user, to settle in the hotel-progression ticket).
- **Cosmetics** (uniform, cab decoration): not in the demo.
